Forecasting Chicago transit ridership

Daily ridership forecasts two weeks ahead for the Chicago Transit Authority (CTA). The model is built from 25 years of boarding counts, daily weather, holidays and city events, and every number below is out-of-sample.

Twenty-five years of ridership

The pandemic cut boardings by more than 80% in spring 2020. Ridership has grown every year since, but the recovery is uneven. Weekend travel is back to about 90% of 2019 levels, while weekday commuting, the backbone of the system, has settled near 70%.

Methods

Data. Daily bus and rail boardings from the City of Chicago open data portal (CTA "Daily Boarding Totals", 2001 onward), joined to daily weather for the Loop from the Open-Meteo historical archive (temperature, feels-like minimum, precipitation, snowfall).

Framing. Direct multi-horizon forecasting. Each training row pairs a forecast origin with a target day 1–14 days later. The target is the log of target-day ridership over the origin's trailing four-week level, so the model learns shapes, not the drifting post-pandemic level.

Features. Weekday, day of year, observed federal holidays (split by CTA service schedule), holiday eves, Thanksgiving weekend, recurring events (Marathon, St. Patrick's parade, Pride), weather, the recent same-weekday profile and four-week momentum.

Models. Two baselines (seasonal naive and a 4-week weekday mean), ridge regression with calendar dummies, and gradient-boosted trees (scikit-learn, 400 trees, depth 4). Training starts July 2022.

Validation. Rolling-origin backtest over the final 12 months. The models are refit monthly on data available at the time. WAPE (total absolute error / total riders) is the headline metric.

Uncertainty. 80% intervals come from out-of-sample residual quantiles per horizon. They are sized on a one-year inner hold-out before each refit (split-conformal style) and reported with their realised coverage.

Limitations. Backtests use observed weather, so a live system would add weather-forecast error. Extreme-cold days, when closures are announced at short notice, and rare holiday configurations are the largest remaining misses.

In the browser. The gradient-boosting model is exported tree by tree to JSON and evaluated in JavaScript for the planner. A unit test holds the JavaScript output to the scikit-learn predictions.
